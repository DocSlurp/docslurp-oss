# Northwind Aerial — FY2026 Annual Report (Excerpt)

## Letter from the Chief Executive Officer

Dear Shareholders,

Fiscal year 2026, which ended March 31, 2026, was the strongest year in Northwind Aerial's history. We grew our network, strengthened our fleet, deepened our commitment to customers, and delivered financial results that reflect the discipline of our teams across the world.

Revenue reached a record $482.6 million, up 31% from $368.4 million in FY2025. Growth was broad-based, driven by higher passenger volumes, stronger premium cabin demand, and the successful introduction of our first transatlantic service. Operating margin expanded to 18.4% from 14.9%, as we spread fixed costs over a larger base, improved aircraft utilization, and benefited from the fuel efficiency of our newest aircraft. Net income was $61.2 million.

These results are a credit to the 3,920 people who make up Northwind Aerial. Their professionalism and care for our customers are the foundation of everything we do.

Priya Raman
Chief Executive Officer

## Strategic Priorities and Progress

Our strategy rests on three pillars: expand into high-value long-haul markets, renew and grow the fleet with efficient aircraft, and earn customer loyalty through a consistently excellent experience. In FY2026 we made measurable progress against each.

### 1. Entering the Transatlantic Market

On June 3, 2025, we launched a daily jet service from Denver to London Heathrow. The route connects one of the fastest-growing business and leisure hubs in the American West with Europe's most important international gateway. Demand exceeded our expectations from the first week. Load factor on the London route averaged 89% in its first nine months, supported by strong corporate bookings, healthy premium cabin uptake, and connecting traffic from our domestic network.

The route has also strengthened our commercial relationships with corporate accounts and travel partners, and it has become a showcase for the product standards we intend to bring to future long-haul markets.

### 2. Fleet Renewal and Growth

During the year Northwind took delivery of 6 new Airbus A321XLR aircraft, bringing the fleet to 54 aircraft. The A321XLR's extended range makes it well suited to our transatlantic ambitions, while its lower fuel burn per seat supports our margin objectives and our environmental commitments. The new aircraft feature our updated cabin, with improved seating, expanded power access, and enhanced connectivity.

Our maintenance and engineering teams integrated the new aircraft smoothly, maintaining strong operational reliability throughout a period of significant expansion.

### 3. A Better Customer Experience

Customer satisfaction, measured by Net Promoter Score (NPS), rose to 64 from 52. Improvements came from more reliable operations, a redesigned mobile app, faster issue resolution through our customer care teams, and the quality of the new cabin. We view NPS as a leading indicator of loyalty and will continue to invest in the service touchpoints customers value most.

## Operating and Financial Review

### Revenue

Record revenue of $482.6 million reflects growth in both capacity and yield. The London route made a meaningful contribution in its first year, while our domestic network benefited from added frequencies and strong demand for premium products. Ancillary revenue, including seat selection, baggage, and loyalty partnerships, grew alongside passenger volumes.

### Profitability

Operating margin expanded to 18.4% from 14.9%, a significant improvement of 3.5 percentage points. Three factors were most important:

- **Scale benefits:** Higher revenue absorbed fixed costs more efficiently.
- **Fleet efficiency:** New A321XLR aircraft reduced fuel consumption per seat.
- **Productivity:** Improved crew scheduling and turnaround processes supported higher aircraft utilization.

Net income was $61.2 million, reflecting the strength of operating performance and continued financial discipline.

### Balance Sheet and Liquidity

We ended the year with a sound balance sheet and ample liquidity to fund our growth plans. Fleet investments were financed through a combination of operating cash flow and long-term financing on attractive terms. We remain committed to a prudent capital structure that supports both investment and shareholder returns.

## Capital Returns

Reflecting confidence in our performance and outlook, the board approved a quarterly dividend of $0.18 per share, first payable on July 15, 2026. This marks the beginning of a regular dividend program, which we expect to balance with continued investment in fleet and network growth. The board will review the dividend periodically in light of results, cash generation, and strategic opportunities.

## Our People

Employee headcount grew to 3,920, as we added pilots, cabin crew, technicians, airport staff, and corporate specialists to support a larger operation and the new London service. We invested in training, including type-rating programs for the A321XLR and expanded leadership development. Our teams also took part in safety and service initiatives that contributed to the improvement in customer satisfaction.

Safety remains our first priority. Our safety management system, supported by open reporting and continuous review, guided every aspect of our growth this year.

## Sustainability

Fleet renewal is the most important lever in our environmental strategy. The A321XLR is more fuel-efficient per seat than the aircraft it complements, helping reduce emissions intensity as we grow. We continue to evaluate sustainable aviation fuel partnerships, ground-operations electrification, and waste reduction in our cabins and facilities. We will report in more detail on these initiatives in our sustainability disclosures.

## Outlook and FY2027 Guidance

We enter FY2027 with momentum. The London route is established, our fleet is younger and more efficient, and our teams are well prepared for continued growth. For FY2027, we expect:

## Risk Considerations

Our business is subject to risks common to the airline industry, including fuel price volatility, economic conditions, regulatory changes, labor markets, weather, and supply chain constraints affecting aircraft and parts. International operations introduce additional exposure to currency movements and geopolitical developments. Management monitors these risks actively and maintains mitigation plans, including fuel and operational contingencies, to protect performance. The forward-looking statements in this excerpt, including FY2027 guidance, are subject to these and other uncertainties.
